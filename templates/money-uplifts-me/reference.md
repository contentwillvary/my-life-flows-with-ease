## Tax and admin by work type

2026/27 rates, England, Wales and Northern Ireland. Keep the one(s) that apply.

**Employed (PAYE)**

- Example at £40,000: income tax about £5,150, NI about £2,190, Plan 2 student loan about £955, 5% pension about £1,690. Take-home is about £2,500 a month.
- Check your tax code each April (1257L is standard) and your payslip once a quarter.
- Ask about **salary sacrifice** for your pension: it also saves NI, and sometimes the employer adds their saving.
- Keep your P60 each year; claim tax relief on work costs you pay yourself.

**Self-employed (sole trader)**

- Register for Self Assessment; the return and any balance are due **31 January** after the tax year, with payments on account in January and July.
- Set aside 25–30% of profit in a separate pot the day money lands (income tax, Class 4 NI at 6% between £12,570 and £50,270, student loan).
- Keep receipts; claim allowable expenses, or the £1,000 trading allowance if your costs are tiny.
- Pension: a personal pension (SIPP) gets basic-rate relief added automatically.

**Limited company director**

- Usually a small salary plus dividends; corporation tax is 19% up to £50,000 profit (26.5% marginal above), due 9 months and a day after year-end.
- Company pension contributions come out of pre-tax profit: often the most tax-efficient way to save.
- Use an accountant; keep the director's loan account clear.

**Side income**

- Trading and property allowances (£1,000 each) and Rent a Room relief (£7,500) can make small side income tax-free. Over those, it goes on a Self Assessment return.

## Definitions

- **Net worth** = everything you own (savings, investments, pension, home value) minus everything you owe (mortgage, loans, cards). Leave the student loan out if you treat it as a tax.
- **Runway** = months your savings would cover essentials with no income.
